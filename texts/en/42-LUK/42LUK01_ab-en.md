## Kapitel 1


### To Theophilus


\v 1-2 Many have already set out to tell the story of what has happened among us, *events God brought to completion*[^1], based on what was handed down to us *in the way teachers transmit to their students*[^2] by those who saw it from the beginning and became servants of the word.
\v 3-4 So it seemed right to me as well, having followed everything carefully from the start, to write it all out in order for you, most excellent Theophilus,*whose support made this possible*[^3], so that you may know the solid ground beneath what you've been taught.

### An Old Priest and an Impossible Promise


\v 5-7 During the reign of Herod, king of Judea, there was a priest named Zechariah who belonged to the priestly division of Abijah[^4]. His wife Elizabeth was also a descendant of Aaron. Both of them were righteous in God's sight, living blamelessly according to all the commandments and requirements of the \nd Lord[^5]. But they had no children. Elizabeth was barren, and both of them were well along in years.
\v 8-10 One day when Zechariah's division was on duty and he was serving as priest before God, he was chosen by lot, *the honor he had waited decades for*[^6], according to the custom of the priesthood, to enter the sanctuary of the \nd Lord and burn incense. The hour of incense had come, and outside, the whole assembly was praying.
\v 11-12 Then an angel of the \nd Lord appeared to him, standing at the right side of the incense altar. When Zechariah saw him, fear overwhelmed him.
**13** But the angel said to him, "Don't be afraid, Zechariah. Your prayer has been heard. Your wife Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you are to name him John.
**14** He will be a joy and delight to you, and many will rejoice because of his birth.
**15** He will be great in the sight of the \nd Lord. He will never drink wine or other fermented drink, and he will be filled with the Holy Spirit even before he is born.
**16** He will bring many of the people of Israel back to the \nd Lord their God.
**17** And he will go before the \nd Lord, in the spirit and power of Elijah, to turn the hearts of parents to their children and the disobedient to the wisdom of the righteous: to make ready a people prepared for the \nd Lord."
**18** Zechariah asked the angel, "How can I be sure of this? I'm an old man, and my wife is well along in years."
**19** The angel answered, "I am Gabriel. I stand in the presence of God, and I've been sent to bring you this good news.
**20** Now you'll be silent. You won't be able to speak until the day this happens, because you didn't believe my words, which will come true exactly when they're supposed to."
\v 21-22 Meanwhile, the people were waiting for Zechariah and wondering why he was taking so long in the sanctuary. When he came out, he couldn't speak to them: *couldn't give them the blessing they were waiting for*[^7]. They realized he had seen a vision, for he kept making signs to them but remained unable to speak.
\v 23-25 When his time of service was over, he went home. After this, his wife Elizabeth became pregnant and for five months stayed in seclusion. "The \nd Lord has done this for me," she said. "He has shown his favor and taken away my disgrace among the people[^8]."

### Mary Says Yes


\v 26-27 When Elizabeth was six months along, God sent the angel Gabriel to Nazareth, a village in Galilee.*Rome had never bothered to learn its name. A peasant town beneath anyone's notice, which is exactly where the Most High chose to begin.*[^9] He came to a young woman pledged in marriage to a man named Joseph, from David's line. The pledge was legally binding, the same as marriage in everything but living together. Her name was Mary. She had never slept with a man.*Everyone in the village would know that a pregnancy now, before Joseph took her into his house, could only mean one thing: betrayal. A betrothed girl found pregnant faced public denunciation, divorce, even death by stoning. Joseph's royal bloodline was no incidental detail: it would carry straight into this child's legal name and inheritance rights, regardless of who fathered him.*[^10]
\v 28-29 The angel came to her and said, "Be glad, favored one.*God has poured out his grace on you, and it rests on you still.* The Lord is with you."[^11] The words shook her. She turned them over and over, trying to make sense of a greeting like that.[^12]
\v 30-33 "Don't be afraid, Mary," the angel said. "God has chosen to show you his favor. Listen: you are going to conceive and give birth to a son, and you are to name him Jesus. He will be great. He will be called the Son of the Most High, and the Lord God will give him the throne of his ancestor David. He will reign over the people of Jacob forever, and his kingdom will never end."*This is the promise sworn to David coming due at last: the throne re-established, now without end.*[^13]
**34** "How can this happen," Mary asked, "when I have never slept with a man?"*Not whether God could be trusted, but how this would actually work. She knew what a pregnancy would cost her, the shame it would bring, the danger. And still she stayed in the conversation.*[^14]
**35** The angel answered, "The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will settle over you,*the way God's presence once filled the tent of meeting. The same glory-cloud that overshadowed the tabernacle now overshadows you, and you become the place where God dwells.* So the child will be holy, and he will be called the Son of God.[^15]
\v 36-37 And here is something more: your relative Elizabeth, the one everyone called barren, is six months pregnant in her old age.*The proof is already breathing, a sign you can go and see for yourself.* No word that comes from God will ever fail."[^16]
**38** "I am the \nd LORD's servant," Mary said.*His to command, by my own choosing and no one else's.* "I give myself to this. Let everything happen to me just as you have said."[^17] And the angel left her.

### Two Women, Two Miracles


\v 39-40 Mary set out right away and hurried into the hill country of Judea *eighty miles south, several days on foot*[^18]. She came to the town where Zechariah lived, entered his house, and greeted Elizabeth.
**41** When Elizabeth heard Mary's greeting, the baby inside her leaped. And Elizabeth, *older, married to a priest*[^19], was filled with the Holy Spirit and cried out in a loud voice:
\v 42-45 "Blessed are you among women *standing with the warriors and deliverers*[^20], and blessed is the child you are carrying! Why me? How is it that the mother of my Lord should come to me? The moment I heard your greeting, the baby inside me leaped for joy! And blessed is she who believed that what the Lord said to her would be fulfilled."

### His Name Is John


\v 57-58 When the time came, Elizabeth gave birth to a son. Word got out: neighbors, relatives, everyone heard how the Lord had poured mercy on her. They came to celebrate.
\v 59-61 On the eighth day *they gathered for the circumcision ceremony, when the child would be formally named and entered into the covenant people*[^21] they all assumed he'd be called Zechariah, after his father. *Naming a son after his father or grandfather honored the family line. Everyone expected it.*[^22]
But Elizabeth cut in. "No. His name is John."
They looked at her, confused. "John? There's no one in your family with that name."
\v 62-63 So they turned to Zechariah, using signs, since he still couldn't speak, asking what *he* wanted to call the child.
He motioned for a writing tablet.[^23] Everyone watched as he wrote: "His name IS John." *The name means "YHWH has shown grace," a summary of everything that had happened.*[^24]
They were stunned.
**64** And right then, immediately, Zechariah's mouth opened. His tongue loosened. The first words out: blessing God.
\v 65-66 Fear came over everyone in the neighborhood. The news spread through the entire Judean hill country. People couldn't stop talking about it. Everyone who heard asked the same question: "What is this child going to become?"
Because clearly, the hand of \nd the LORD[^25],*the power and favor of God himself,*[^26] was with him.

### Growing Up in the Wilderness


**80** The child grew up, and his spirit grew strong. He lived out in the wilderness *where prophets are formed*[^27] until the day he appeared publicly to Israel.

## Anmerkungen

[^1]: TEXT: The Greek perfect tense (πεπληροφορημένων) emphasizes divine fulfillment and completion, not merely historical occurrence.
[^2]: KULTUR: In both Jewish and Greco-Roman education, authoritative teaching was formally transmitted through recognized chains from teacher to student.
[^3]: KULTUR: The honorific "most excellent" (κράτιστε) indicates high social standing. Literary works were typically funded by wealthy patrons; dedicating a work honored both writer and recipient.
[^4]: KULTUR: The course of Abijah was the eighth of twenty-four priestly divisions (1 Chronicles 24:10), each serving one week twice yearly plus major festivals (Bovon, Hermeneia; Fitzmyer, Anchor).
[^5]: NAME: LORD, in small capitals, represents God's personal name, YHWH, Yahweh.
[^6]: KULTUR: Priestly divisions served one week twice yearly; being chosen by lot to offer incense was a once-in-a-lifetime privilege most priests never received (Bovon, Hermeneia; Bock, Baker).
[^7]: KULTUR: The priest emerging from the Holy Place would pronounce the Aaronic blessing (Numbers 6:24-26) over the assembly; Zechariah's silence meant this expected benediction could not be given (Bock, Baker).
[^8]: HARM: Elizabeth's "disgrace" (ὄνειδος, oneidos) was social stigma imposed by others, never divine judgment on her character; the narrative vindicates her as righteous (v. 6) (Bovon, Hermeneia).
[^9]: **KONTEXT** — *Rome had never bothered to learn its name*: Nazareth was a small agricultural village beneath the notice of Rome and largely of Jerusalem. Luke's reversal theme is already running: the supreme God selects a nobody from nowhere, not a royal house in a capital.
[^10]: **KONTEXT** — *The pledge was legally binding ... royal bloodline*: Betrothal was legally binding, broken only by divorce; the family's honor was at stake. Legal paternity through Joseph conveyed inheritance and lineage rights regardless of biological generation, which is how the child receives the Davidic claim (2 Sam 7). A pregnant betrothed girl faced suspicion of adultery, a capital offense under the Law, though actual executions were rare under Roman rule. Joseph's options included public denunciation or quiet divorce. Mary's yes was socially dangerous.
[^11]: **TEXT** — *favored one ... it rests on you still*: "Favored one" renders the perfect passive participle kecharitomene: a completed action with an ongoing result. Mary has been graced and remains so. Origen noted this exact greeting is found nowhere else in Scripture, addressed to Mary alone.
[^12]: **TEXT** — *The words shook her*: The verb dietarachthe denotes deep inner turmoil, not mild surprise, and dielogizeto pictures her reasoning the greeting through, an interior weighing rather than passive confusion.
[^13]: **KONTEXT** — *his kingdom will never end*: The piled titles—Son of the Most High, throne of David, an everlasting reign—announce the fulfillment of God's covenant promise to David (2 Sam 7). To a first hearer these were not generic honorifics but the long-awaited Messianic claim.
[^14]: **TEXT** — *How can this happen*: Mary's question is inquiry, not doubt. She asks "how?" not "how can I be sure?" In contrast to Zechariah, her question is met with explanation rather than silencing. The illumination captures the social cost she understood: pregnancy during betrothal created suspicion of adultery, and Mary's question reflects awareness of the social impossibility, not biological ignorance.
[^15]: **KONTEXT** — *the way God's presence once filled the tent of meeting*: "Will settle over you" renders episkiasei, "overshadow," echoing the cloud that covered the tent of meeting so that the glory of the \nd LORD\nd* filled it (Exod 40:35). Mary becomes the new locus of divine presence, and the Spirit, not human agency, is the agent of conception.
[^16]: **KONTEXT** — *six months pregnant in her old age*: Elizabeth's pregnancy is offered as a confirming sign. Faith here is not blind: the impossible has already begun in someone Mary knows, answering the unspoken question of how anyone could believe such news.
[^17]: **TEXT** — *I am the LORD's servant*: Doule is a female bondservant, but here it is Mary's own self-designation, a freely chosen radical availability to God, not subservience imposed by men. "Let everything happen to me" renders an optative of willing consent: a powerful active yes, her agency, not passive compliance. The text has been weaponized to enforce female subservience; Mary's virtue is her faith-response, not merely physical state.
[^18]: **KONTEXT:** The journey from Nazareth to the Judean hill country was approximately 80-100 miles and would have taken several days. Bovon, Hermeneia; archaeological evidence from Ein Karem region.
[^19]: **KULTUR:** In ancient Near Eastern culture, age and priestly family status conferred honor. Elizabeth's deference to the younger Mary inverts normal social patterns. Bovon, Hermeneia; Malina & Rohrbaugh, Social-Science Commentary.
[^20]: **KULTUR:** The phrase "blessed among women" echoes blessings of Jael (Judges 5:24) and Judith (Judith 13:18), Israel's heroic women who delivered the people. Bovon, Hermeneia.
[^21]: **KULTUR** Circumcision on the eighth day (Leviticus 12:3) was the sign of covenant membership; naming occurred during this ceremony. Source: Fitzmyer, *Anchor*, 385.
[^22]: **KONTEXT** First-century Jewish families typically named sons after male relatives to preserve family identity and honor. Source: Bovon, *Hermeneia*, 72.
[^23]: **KONTEXT** A πινακίδιον (pinakidion) was a small wooden tablet with a wax surface for temporary writing. Source: BDAG 813.
[^24]: **NAME** Greek Ἰωάννης (Iōannēs), from Hebrew יוֹחָנָן (Yohanan), "YHWH has shown grace" or "YHWH is gracious." Source: BDAG 465; Fitzmyer, *Anchor*, 386.
[^25]: **NAME** LORD in small capitals represents the divine name YHWH (Yahweh).
[^26]: **TEXT** "Hand of the LORD" (χεὶρ κυρίου) is an Old Testament idiom for divine power, presence, and protection (cf. Exodus 9:3; 1 Samuel 5:6; Isaiah 59:1). Source: BDAG 1082; Louw-Nida 76.4.
[^27]: Moses, Elijah, and Israel itself were all formed in wilderness places before their public ministries. (KULTUR: Fitzmyer, Bock)

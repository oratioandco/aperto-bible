## Kapitel 21


### The Widow's Offering


**1** He looked up. *Wealthy donors were filing past the collection chests, dropping in their gifts — the kind that rang against the metal.*
**2** Then he saw a widow. *Destitute.* She dropped in two tiny copper coins.
[^1]

**3** "Listen," he said *to his disciples*. "This woman put in more than all of them combined.
**4** They all gave out of their overflow*— what they had left over*. She gave out of *what she lacked —*everything she had to live on."
[^2]
[^3]
[^4]
[^5]

### Temple Destruction Foretold


**5** Some people were admiring the Temple — *the fine stones, the gifts that generations had dedicated to God*. He said, "These things you're gazing at?
**6** Days are coming when not one stone will be left on another. All of it — torn down."
[^6]
[^7]

**7** "Teacher," they asked, "when? And what will the sign be?"

**8** "Watch that no one leads you astray," he said. "Many will come in my name claiming, 'I am the one!' and 'The time has come!' Do not follow them.
[^8]
**9** And when you hear of wars and revolts — do not be terrified. All that has to happen first. But it's not the end."
[^9]

### Signs, Persecution, and Testimony


**10** Then he said to them, "Nation will rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom.
**11** There will be earthquakes — famines in one place, plagues in another — terrors, and great signs blazing from the sky.

**12** But before any of this — they will seize you and persecute you, handing you over to synagogues and prisons, dragging you before kings and governors because of my name.
**13** This will turn into your chance to testify.
**14** So settle it in your hearts: do not rehearse your defense ahead of time.
**15** I myself will give you words and wisdom that none of your opponents will be able to resist or refute.
[^10]

**16** You will be handed over even by your own parents, your brothers, your relatives, your friends. They will put some of you to death.
**17** Everyone will hate you because of my name.

**18** And yet — not a hair of your head will be lost.

**19** By your endurance you will win yourselves back."
[^11]
[^12]

### Jerusalem Surrounded


**20** "When you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, know this: her devastation has drawn near.
[^13]
**21** Then let those in Judea flee to the mountains. Those inside the city — get out. Those in the countryside — do not go in.
**22** Because these are days of judgment — *fulfilling everything written by the prophets*.
[^14]

**23** And the pregnant women — the mothers still nursing — God help them in those days. There will be terrible distress across the land, and wrath against this people.
**24** They will fall by the edge of the sword. They will be dragged off as prisoners to every nation. And Jerusalem will be trampled by the nations — until the times of the Gentiles have run their course."
[^15]
[^16]

### The Coming of the Son of Man


**25** "There will be signs — in the sun, the moon, the stars. On earth, nations in anguish, bewildered by the roaring and surging of the sea.
[^17]
**26** People collapsing with dread at what is bearing down on the world — the very powers of the heavens shaken loose.
[^18]

**27** And then they will see the Son of Man coming in a cloud, with power and great glory.
[^19]

**28** When these things begin to happen — stand tall. Lift your heads. Your liberation is drawing near."
[^20]

### The Parable of the Fig Tree


**29** *He told them a parable:* "Look at the fig tree — *any tree*.
**30** When fresh leaves appear, you know without being told: summer is coming.
**31** In the same way, when you see these things taking shape, know this — the kingdom of God is close.

**32** Truly I tell you: this generation will not pass away until all of it has taken place.
[^21]
[^22]

**33** Heaven and earth will pass away. But my words — never."
[^23]

### Watchfulness


**34** "Guard your hearts. Don't let them get heavy with overindulgence and drunkenness and the anxieties of ordinary life —
**35** or that day will close on you like a trap*, every last person on earth*.
[^24]

**36** Stay alert. Pray at every turn — for the strength to escape what is coming, and to stand before the Son of Man."
[^25]

**37** *During the days,* he taught in the Temple. At night he went out and stayed on the hill called the Mount of Olives.
**38** And *the people — all of them —* would rise early every morning and come to the Temple to listen.

## Anmerkungen

[^1]: **KULTUR** — *two tiny copper coins*: Two λεπτά (lepta) — the smallest coins in circulation, each worth 1/128 of a denarius. Together they equaled one quadrans: not enough to buy a single loaf of bread. The treasury was in the Court of Women, where thirteen trumpet-shaped (shofar) collection chests amplified the sound of coins falling in. The wealthy made the treasury ring; her two coins barely registered.
[^2]: **TEXT** — *everything she had to live on*: The Greek βίος means "livelihood" or "survival budget" — not vague "possessions." This was her next meal. The word choice makes the stakes concrete: she gave away her physical survival, not an abstract portion of wealth.
[^3]: **KONTEXT** — *devour widows' houses*: This scene follows directly after 20:45-47, where Jesus condemns the scribes who "devour widows' houses." The juxtaposition is deliberate: Jesus has just described the predators — now he observes their prey. The same Temple system he condemned is receiving this widow's last coins.
[^4]: **UNGLAUBLICH** — *Is this praise — or lament?*: Three readings: (1) Traditional: Jesus praises the widow's extraordinary faith and generosity — she gives everything, trusting God completely. (2) Critical: Jesus laments a system that takes a destitute woman's survival budget — the passage is an indictment, not a commendation. (3) Both: Jesus recognizes the greatness of her act while grieving the system that demands it. The text itself does not resolve the tension. "Listen" (v.3) is neutral — not approving, not condemning. The reader must hold both possibilities.
[^5]: **LEBEN** — *giving beyond their means*: When does generosity become exploitation? This passage has been used to guilt economically vulnerable people into giving beyond their means. The widow's act is presented as remarkable precisely because it is extraordinary — not as a standard to impose. If a religious institution's funding depends on destitute people giving away their survival budget, the system Jesus condemned in 20:47 is still operating.
[^6]: **KULTUR** — *the fine stones*: Herod's Temple was one of the ancient world's architectural marvels: massive white limestone blocks (some weighing over 500 tons), a golden vine over the entrance, and votive offerings accumulated over centuries. Josephus (Antiquities 15.394-95) records that the Temple's white stone and gold "reflected so fierce a blaze of fire that the eye was forced to turn away." The disciples' admiration was the normal response. Jesus' prediction of total destruction was scandalous.
[^7]: **UNGLAUBLICH** — *Was this prophecy fulfilled?*: In 70 CE, Roman legions under Titus destroyed the Temple completely. Josephus (Jewish War 7.1-4) describes the systematic demolition: "Caesar ordered the whole city and the Temple to be razed to the ground." Archaeological evidence confirms the destruction — massive stones from the Temple platform have been found where they fell. The prophecy "not one stone upon another" describes observed historical reality.
[^8]: **TEXT** — *I am the one!*: The Greek Ἐγώ εἰμι ("I am") echoes the divine name revealed to Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3:14, LXX). The false claimants are not merely saying "I'm the messiah" — they are appropriating the language of divine self-revelation. The gravity of the deception is proportional to the language they steal.
[^9]: **LEBEN** — *it's not the end*: Jesus explicitly says that wars and political upheavals are NOT the signal for the end — "it's not the end." This verse directly undermines every attempt to read current events as proof that the End is imminent. Every generation has had its wars, revolts, and crises. Jesus' instruction is against credulity and date-setting, not in favor of it.
[^10]: **KONTEXT** — *words and wisdom*: "I will give you a mouth and wisdom" places Jesus in the role of the divine speech-provider — the tradition of Moses ("I will be with your mouth," Exodus 4:12) and Jeremiah ("I have put my words in your mouth," Jeremiah 1:9). The early church understood this as fulfilled in the eloquence of martyrs before Roman courts: Polycarp, the Scillitan martyrs, Perpetua.
[^11]: **TEXT** — *win yourselves back*: Two issues: (1) The Greek ψυχάς means "selves/lives" as integrated persons — NOT "souls" in a body-soul dualist sense. The self is what endurance wins back. (2) A significant textual variant exists: κτήσασθε (aorist imperative — "Win yourselves back!") vs. κτήσεσθε (future indicative — "You will win yourselves back"). The first is a command; the second is a promise. Both are textually well attested. Either way, the verb κτάομαι means active acquisition: the self is won through faithfulness, not automatically preserved.
[^12]: **LEBEN** — *endure your suffering*: This passage addresses persecution by political authorities (synagogues, prisons, kings, governors) — imperial state violence against a minority community. It has been misused to counsel silence in the face of institutional church abuse: "endure your suffering for Christ." Luke 21:19 calls for testimony (v.13), not silence. Persecution by the state and abuse within a religious community are categorically different situations.
[^13]: **KONTEXT** — *Jerusalem surrounded by armies*: Luke's most significant editorial decision in the entire discourse. Mark 13:14 has "the abomination of desolation standing where it ought not (let the reader understand)" — a Danielic code phrase (Daniel 9:27) requiring insider knowledge to decode. Luke replaces this entirely with plain military language: "Jerusalem surrounded by armies." This makes the prophecy historically actionable and verifiable — no apocalyptic decoder ring required.
[^14]: **TEXT** — *days of judgment*: The Greek ἡμέραι ἐκδικήσεως literally means "days of vindication/retribution." English "vengeance" sounds personally vindictive; the Greek describes divine judicial action — God acting as judge through historical events. The destruction of Jerusalem is framed as fulfilling prophetic warnings (Jeremiah 7; Deuteronomy 28), not as divine spite.
[^15]: **KULTUR** — *the times of the Gentiles*: Luke's unique phrase (καιροὶ ἐθνῶν) — found nowhere else in the New Testament. Three major interpretive options: (1) The period of Gentile political domination over Jerusalem, ending at the parousia; (2) The period of Gentile mission and conversion (cf. Romans 11:25, "the fullness of the Gentiles"); (3) The providential time allotted to Gentile powers before God's final intervention. The crucial word is "until" (ἄχρι) — whatever the "times" are, they have a divinely appointed limit. This phrase does NOT endorse any modern political arrangement regarding Jerusalem.
[^16]: **APOLOGETICS** — *fall by the edge of the sword*: Josephus (Jewish War 6-7) confirms the 70 CE fulfillment with remarkable specificity: approximately 1.1 million killed during the siege, 97,000 taken captive (many sent to arena deaths, mines, and slavery throughout the empire). The famine was so severe that Josephus records cases of cannibalism. The phrase "by the edge of the sword" matches his account of mass slaughter; "dragged off as prisoners to every nation" matches the mass deportation.
[^17]: **KONTEXT** — *signs in the sun, moon, stars*: Cosmic disruption language — sun, moon, stars shaken — is conventional Old Testament prophetic vocabulary for divine intervention, not astronomical prediction. Joel 2:10, 31 ("the sun will be turned to darkness and the moon to blood"); Isaiah 13:10 ("the stars of heaven will not show their light"); Ezekiel 32:7-8 (similar imagery for the fall of Egypt). Ancient listeners heard prophetic poetry, not science fiction.
[^18]: **UNGLAUBLICH** — *powers of the heavens shaken*: Cosmic disruption — sun darkened, moon failing, stars falling, heavenly powers shaken — is the hardest category for modern readers. Three readings: (1) Literal: actual astronomical and cosmic events preceding the end of history — the universe physically coming apart. (2) Prophetic poetry: conventional Old Testament imagery for God intervening in history (Joel 2:10, Isaiah 13:10, Ezekiel 32:7), understood by ancient audiences as dramatic theological language, not a weather forecast. When Isaiah says the stars "will not show their light" at Babylon's fall (13:10), no one expects literal stellar failure. (3) Symbolic-political: "powers of the heavens" as the spiritual forces behind earthly empires — Rome's cosmic order collapsing before God's kingdom. The text does not choose between these. What it does do is reverse the expected emotional response: while the nations faint with terror, Jesus tells his followers to stand tall. Whatever these signs are, they are not cause for dread but for hope.
[^19]: **KONTEXT** — *the Son of Man coming in a cloud*: Daniel 7:13-14: "One like a son of man coming with the clouds of heaven... given dominion and glory and a kingdom." In Daniel, the "son of man" figure represents the faithful community who receive the kingdom after the empires (depicted as beasts) are judged. Jesus identifies himself as this figure. Note Luke's singular "cloud" (ἐν νεφέλῃ) — the specific cloud of divine presence (the Shekinah, Exodus 13:21), not generic atmospheric phenomenon.
[^20]: **TEXT** — *Stand tall. Lift your heads. Your liberation*: Three translation decisions: (1) ἀνακύψατε — BDAG: "take heart in expectation of deliverance." Not merely "look up" but the posture of the freed, standing tall. (2) ἐπάρατε τὰς κεφαλάς — the oppressed look down; the liberated look up. This is a physical-spiritual posture command. (3) ἀπολύτρωσις — from slave-buying language: the price paid to release a person from captivity. Luke's only use of this word. The parousia is not judgment for the persecuted community — it is liberation day.
[^21]: **TEXT** — *this generation*: The most contested verse in Luke's eschatology. Four major interpretive options: (a) Jesus' contemporaries — the generation alive in ~30 CE; "all things" refers to the 70 CE events (most linguistically natural reading); (b) The Jewish people as a nation — γενεά as "race/people" (rare usage); (c) An evil/faithless generation type that will persist (no contextual support); (d) The final generation alive at the eschaton (conflicts with the demonstrative "this"). The partial preterist solution: "all things" in v.32 refers to vv.20-24 (historically fulfilled in 70 CE), while vv.25-28 (cosmic events) remain future. This preserves both historical fulfillment and still-future parousia.
[^22]: **LEBEN** — *this generation*: This verse has been repeatedly weaponized for end-time date-setting with documented pastoral harm: Hal Lindsey (1970s: "this generation" = generation of 1948, therefore End before 1988), Harold Camping (2011), and countless others. Jesus himself refused to answer "when?" (v.7 → v.8's response is "don't be fooled," not a date). Using a contested verse to set dates directly contradicts the discourse's own instruction.
[^23]: **UNGLAUBLICH** — *my words will never pass away*: The most radical authority claim in the chapter — and perhaps in the entire Gospel. Jesus places his own words above the permanence of the cosmos. Three readings: (1) Christological: Jesus speaks with divine authority; his words share the permanence of God's own speech. (2) Prophetic: Like the Torah and the Prophets, Jesus' teaching will endure because it carries divine truth. (3) Skeptical: This is a later theological attribution, placing cosmic authority claims on the lips of the historical Jesus. The claim is either the most extraordinary statement ever made, or the most presumptuous. The text presents it without qualification.
[^24]: **LEBEN** — *the anxieties of ordinary life*: Notice the syntactic parallel: dissipation AND drunkenness AND daily anxieties. The third item is listed alongside moral vices — not as a lesser concern but as an equally dangerous spiritual condition. Luke is not condemning people who struggle with anxiety. He is warning that the daily grind of worry can capture the heart as effectively as excess and addiction. Both the comfortable (in danger from overindulgence) and the precarious (in danger from anxiety) need the same watchfulness. Eschatological vigilance requires freedom from both.
[^25]: **KONTEXT** — *to stand before the Son of Man*: This is the goal of the entire discourse. The widow stood before the treasury (v.1). The persecuted stand before kings and governors (v.12). Now the faithful stand before the Son of Man — not cowering, not fleeing, but standing firm (σταθῆναι, from ἵστημι). The posture is confidence in the presence of the liberator, echoing v.28's "stand tall, lift your heads." The chapter that began with a woman giving everything ends with a community standing in the presence of everything.
